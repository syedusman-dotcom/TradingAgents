## What the evidence says, in one paragraph

Nothing this tooling can compute from accounts and prices has beaten the S&P 500 on US large and mid caps since 2010: the current value rule, the Magic Formula, the quality-record filters, expected-return hurdles, trend and momentum overlays, small caps, and a new quality-plus-momentum rule all come out at or below SPY once survivorship and a hold-out are respected (ledger S1, S3, S4 and the 2026-11-05 buy-bar runs). The agents' 153 reviews produced no Buy because their own values sit within a few percent of price (median margin of safety −5%), which is what a fairly priced market looks like, and published evidence gives LLM analysts no edge after their training cut-off. The only strategy a Nordnet customer can buy with a live, net, out-of-sample record of beating its index is European momentum (about +1.8 to +2 points a year over MSCI Europe since 2015, decaying, t ≈ 0.9 alone); Swedish investment companies roughly tie the S&P 500 in kronor. A 5–10 stock satellite beats the index less than half the time by construction, and a return-based proof of a 2-point edge would take decades, so the process can only be judged by the calibration of its probability statements and by paper records. The honest expected excess return of a disciplined private stock-picking process is about zero, within −2 to +2 points.

## The process

### 1. Core
The Nordnet USA Index fund (the user's choice on 2026-11-06, no commission, 0.20% a year) holds every krona not in a stock position. Idle cash costs more than a zero-edge satellite (R3: about 0.4–0.5% a year for 10% idle), so `parking.share` stays at 0.9 and the fund is sold only to fund a buy. Unchanged.

### 2. Satellites
- **Stock satellite (exists).** The agent review process picks single stocks under the rules in section 3. Its size is the open question in section 5.
- **European momentum ETF (recommendation b).** iShares Edge MSCI Europe Momentum Factor (CEMR, 0.25%) at 10–15% of the account is the one addition with evidence (R1). Pre-registered falsifier: sell it if it trails MSCI Europe by more than 5 points over any rolling three years from purchase, or if the 10-year edge in MSCI's factsheet falls below +1 point. Expected contribution to the whole account: about +0.1 to +0.3 points a year. The user decides; the tooling does not model ETFs beyond the parking fund today, so a position would be recorded like any other ticker.
- **Swedish investment companies (recommendation b, weak).** Investor, Industrivärden or Spiltan Aktiefond Investmentbolag give krona diversification at about the S&P 500's return in SEK; 2–2.5 points a year of Investor's past lead was its discount closing and it now trades at a premium. Not a return argument; a currency one.

### 3. The stock review, as it runs now
Screen (Magic Formula rank, quality filters, lane hints) → candidates (value lane from `review.candidate_countries`, compounder and dislocation hints from every market) → eleven agents (fundamentals with the worksheet, news, bull/bear, three risk views, a Portfolio Manager with the four-test checklist and scenarios) → an investment committee → ACTIONS with the rules' sizing. Rating bars: Buy needs the three quality tests, a 30% margin of safety against the scenario value and upside/downside of 3; Overweight 15% and 2; the compounder lane has its own price bars. **Unchanged, by evidence:** lowering the bar has no support (`value_graded` at 20% margin: −1.9 and −2.9 points over SPY; the paper Buys at 0% margin are the live test of a looser rule, first verdict about June 2027).

What changed this weekend, all (a):
- **Data the agents read.** The worksheet now has a formula value for every company whose quote currency differs from its statement currency (Betsson, Celestica, most Nordic and Swiss names), so the value bridge has a start (commit b823beb). The pipeline note carries the screen's F-Score, Altman Z, Beneish M, accounting flags and formula value (331c49c). The committee reads whole theses (331c49c). News no longer depends on Yahoo alone: Nordic exchange releases (Nasdaq Stockholm/Helsinki/Copenhagen, Oslo Børs NewsWeb) come first for `.ST/.HE/.CO/.OL` names, then Yahoo, then a keyless Google News sample (fabfc11, 3aee834). Insider transactions for Swedish names come from Finansinspektionen's register and for Finnish, Danish and Norwegian names from the exchanges' managers'-transaction notices; Yahoo's were empty or zero-filled for all of them (3aee834). A third of the management-test failures were "not proven" for lack of exactly this data (A1).
- **Measurement.** Brier scores of the agents' bear and bull probabilities against a base-rate forecaster, on the calibration page and in the `calibration` command, with the reading rule fixed before any data: 30 decisions at 365 days; informative when both skill scores are above 0; over-confident when stated exceeds realised by more than 10 points (1a94128). This is the only signal the literature says a process this size can read within one to three years (R2).
- **Research tools.** `pullback-backtest` (fefff21), `backtest --leavers` (55b4fe0, pinned Wikipedia revision), `record_momentum` candidate (ac80802).

### 4. Entry and exit rules
- **Entry price queue (keep, a).** The committee's buy queue (buy only at or below the review's entry price, money in the index meanwhile) was tested on 2015–2025 formula picks: waiting for a 10% dip and otherwise staying in the index was as good as or better than buying at once for every rule, even though the names that never dipped were the best performers (ledger S2; fixed verdict "earns its keep"). Published evidence on limit orders says the opposite for the market as a whole (R3: waiting for a 10% correction 6.6% a year vs 12.1% buying at once, 1990–2024); the two reconcile because the unfilled money here sits in the index, not cash, and formula picks carry no edge to forgo. The one lane with alpha in the backtest shows the cost the limit-order studies predict: the strict compounder picks made +7.6 points bought at once, +7.1 with a 10% dip and +4.9 with a 15% dip (R3's reading of S2). So: keep the entry-price queue for value and dislocation candidates, and let a compounder-lane Buy be bought near the market price (the lane's own price bar already limits what it pays). The second part is a rule change that the committee's prompt and `actions` must carry; it is listed in section 6 as part of the next agent round, not applied blind. Entry prices expire with the review that set them (a re-review resets them), which is the 90–120-day expiry R3 suggests in practice.
- **Kill criteria (keep, a).** Measurable fundamental thresholds, 3–6 per position, checked in the weekly watch and at each review. R3 adds: a breached criterion means a sale by default unless a written rebuttal is filed within one review cycle; no price stops on quality or value holdings (stops lower returns under a random walk, tight ones always); trim back to the cap with a band rather than exit; full re-verification after each quarterly report. These are already how the Portfolio Manager and the watch are written; the rebuttal rule is a prompt line to add in the next agent round (b, below).
- **Selling counterfactual (follow-up).** Log every sell against a random-sell benchmark (institutions' sells trail a random sell by about 0.8% a year); a one-line addition to the calibration page once there are sells to score.

### 5. Sizing — the decisions for the user (b)
Evidence (R3): equal weight beats every optimiser in practice; the odds a 5-stock portfolio beats the market are 47% over one year and 41% over ten with no skill; the satellite's edge-to-tracking-error ratio does not depend on its size, so a small satellite loses little if the edge is zero and proves nothing faster if it is not; 8–10 names diversify better than 5.
1. **Cap the stock satellite at 20% of the account until the first paper-Buy verdict (June 2027) and the one-year review against SPY (2027-10-24).** Today's rules allow 10% per stock with a five-stock minimum, which can take the satellite past 50%. My recommendation: positions of 5% (half the current size), at most four, equal weight, trimmed at 7%. If the paper Buys and the Brier reading both come out positive, raise it.
2. **Compounder Overweights.** The only name the process wanted to buy since October (Deckers, Overweight, 15% margin, upside/downside 2.4, no red flags, committee BUY twice) was blocked by the setting `sizing.lane_buy_only: [compounder]` (A1). That setting rests on the first, yearly backtest of 2026-10-24 (compounder Overweights −2.5 points over SPY on eight cohorts); the quarterly runs since are mixed and thin (1-year median −2.0, 3-year +5.6 over 2015–25; +16.8 on the 2013–14 hold-out with seven cohorts), so the evidence neither supports nor rejects them. Recommendation, weakly held: allow them at half size inside the satellite cap, so the live record contains the process's own best ideas rather than nothing; the cost of being wrong is bounded by the cap. The user chose the strict setting on 2026-10-24, so reversing it is theirs to decide.
3. **The 5-stock minimum** becomes a target of 8–10 names at the satellite's full size, not a reason to buy Hold-rated stocks (the committee already refuses that).

### 6. The agents — next round (b, with a measurement plan)
R2's evidence-backed changes to the review itself, in order: (i) a price-blind valuation pass — the fundamentals analyst values the company before it sees the price, then the Portfolio Manager compares; (ii) the bear analyst gets its own document search and the judge reads the two cases in random order (debate among same-model agents converges on the majority 58–86% of the time and does not beat averaging independent views); (iii) independent first views from the three risk analysts before they see each other; (iv) scenario probabilities anchored on base rates (Mauboussin: 41% of top-ROIC companies still top-quintile nine years later), with the LLM's margins treated as optimistic; (v) the kill-criterion rebuttal rule. None was applied this weekend: they change the agents' behaviour and could not be tested without spending the Fable quota reserved for the scheduled reviews. Measurement: run the changed graph on the same candidates as the unchanged one for one month, compare Brier readings at 180 days and the share of "not provided / not retrieved" lines; adopt the change only if it does not worsen calibration.

### 7. What was rejected, with the number
- Trend/breakout sleeve: −4.8% a year vs SPY 13.6% (2026-11-05). Momentum stock sleeve with the fixed rule: 10.2% vs 13.6%, drawdown 46% (2026-11-05). Momentum funds track their parents (MTUM = SPY over the period).
- S&P 600 Magic Formula: −5.0 points vs SPY, 8 of 43 cohorts (2026-11-06). Nordic small caps: untestable; live via paper Buys.
- Expected-return hurdles, trending value, value without downtrend, compounder FCF, quality + momentum (`record_momentum`: −1.5 over SPY, 10 of 43 cohorts): none robust (S1, S4).
- Quality ETFs: tie before fees, trail after. Equal weight, minimum volatility, dividend aristocrats, buyback funds: all trailed 2010–2026. Concentrated compounder funds: gave back their lead after 2021; 97% of European and 100% of Nordic active funds trailed over ten years (R1).
- Lowering the Buy bar: no rule at any margin shows an edge; the paper Buys will say whether "good business at a fair price" pays by mid-2027.
- A historical backtest of the agents themselves: contaminated by training-data leakage (R2); only the forward record counts.

### 8. How the process is judged
- Brier reading on the calibration page (30 decisions at 365 days; first reading late 2027, early read at 180 days from spring 2027).
- Paper Buys verdict (≥ 20 counted, ≥ 3 months, mean excess ≥ +3 points, ≥ 55% ahead): about June 2027.
- One-year review of the account against SPY: 2027-10-24, with the satellite and the core reported separately.
- The user's own decisions in section 5 recorded in the ledger with the date, so the record cannot be rewritten later.
